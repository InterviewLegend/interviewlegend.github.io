A roadmap for the software engineer interview

Most prep advice is a pile of problem links with no order to it. This is the order that actually works — coding patterns first, then design, then the human rounds, then the company you're actually targeting. Every step points to a free, in-depth breakdown.

Who this is for: software engineers preparing for interviews at big tech and beyond, who want a path instead of a random problem list. The resources below are free and go deep on each stage. Maintained by the team behind InterviewLegend.

1Build pattern recognition before volume

The single biggest mistake is grinding hundreds of random problems. Interviews reward recognizing which pattern a problem wants — two pointers, sliding window, BFS/DFS, dynamic programming, intervals, heaps — not memorizing individual solutions. Learn the patterns on a curated set first, then scale volume only where you're weak.

Start here: Is Blind 75 enough? — why the 75-problem list is the efficient floor, and where it stops short. And the full data structures & algorithms foundations.

2Know the format that grades you before a human does

Many companies gate the loop behind an automated assessment — a timed, auto-scored coding test — before anyone reads your resume. The scoring logic is nothing like a live interview, so it needs its own prep: speed, hidden test cases, and edge coverage over elegance.

Read: How the CodeSignal assessment is scored and what the Amazon Online Assessment actually tests.

3Learn system design as a language, not a checklist

For mid-level and up, system design often decides the offer. It isn't about memorizing architectures — it's about reasoning through requirements, tradeoffs, and scale out loud. Build the core vocabulary (load balancing, caching, partitioning, consistency) and practice narrating decisions.

Read: The system design questions companies actually ask.

4Treat the behavioral round as a scored round

Strong coders get cut here constantly, because they wing it. The behavioral round is graded — on judgment, ownership, and how you handle conflict and failure. The fix is a small bank of specific stories, structured so the interviewer can actually follow them.

Read: The behavioral questions software engineers get, the STAR method done right, and how to open with "tell me about yourself". For Amazon specifically: the Leadership Principles and the Bar Raiser; for Google, Googleyness & Leadership.

5Layer company-specific prep on top

This is the step almost everyone skips, and it's where generic prep loses to targeted prep. Google weights different patterns than Amazon; Meta's design bar differs from Netflix's; a bank's loop looks nothing like a startup's. Once your fundamentals are solid, study the exact process, question style, and scoring rubric of the company you're actually interviewing with.
